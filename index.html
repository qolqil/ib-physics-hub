Common Misconceptions — Q&A

"Centripetal force is a real force that acts on an object in circular motion." +
No — centripetal force is not a new force. It's the net inward force provided by real forces (tension, gravity, normal force, etc.). Never draw a "Fc" arrow on a free-body diagram as a separate force.
"Objects in free fall have no gravitational force acting on them." +
Wrong. Gravity acts on them — that's why they accelerate. "Weightless" means the normal force is zero, not that gravity is absent. Astronauts in orbit are in continuous free fall.
"Heat and temperature are the same thing." +
They're not. Temperature is a measure of average kinetic energy of particles (intensive). Heat (Q) is the transfer of thermal energy between objects (extensive, measured in Joules).
"A heavier object falls faster than a lighter one." +
False in a vacuum. All objects experience the same gravitational acceleration (g = 9.8 m/s²) regardless of mass. Air resistance changes this in practice, which is a different problem.
"In SHM, the period depends on the amplitude of oscillation." +
Not for ideal SHM. The period T = 2π√(m/k) for a spring and T = 2π√(l/g) for a pendulum — neither includes amplitude. This is only valid for small amplitudes in the pendulum case.
"Electric current is used up as it flows through a circuit." +
Current (charge per second) is the same everywhere in a series circuit. What gets "used up" is energy. The potential difference (voltage) drops across components, not the current.

Exam Strategy

Paper formats, timing, and what actually costs marks — based on examiner reports.

1A
Multiple Choice
SL: 30 questions · 45 min  |  HL: 40 questions · 60 min
  • Allow ~1.5 min per question. Flag and move on if stuck — return at end.
  • Eliminate obviously wrong answers first, then choose from what remains.
  • Watch for "all of the above" and "none of the above" — read every option.
  • Units are clues — if the answer needs m/s and only one option has those units, that's it.
  • Always answer every question — no penalty for guessing.
1B
Short Answer (Data-Based)
SL: 35 marks · 55 min  |  HL: 45 marks · 75 min
  • Allocate ~1.5 min per mark. A 3-mark question = ~4.5 minutes max.
  • Show substitution of values even for simple calculations — it's a mark.
  • State the equation you're using before substituting.
  • For graph questions: label axes with units, plot points accurately, draw best-fit line through the error bars.
  • Uncertainty propagation: add absolute uncertainties for + and −; add % uncertainties for × and ÷.
2
Extended Response
SL: 50 marks · 75 min  |  HL: 90 marks · 135 min
  • 1.5 min per mark. Section A is compulsory; Section B has choice — pick your strongest topic.
  • "Explain" = mechanism + physics principle + why. One sentence is never enough.
  • "Outline" = just the key point, no elaboration needed.
  • "Discuss" = both sides or multiple factors with evidence.
  • For 6+ mark questions: structure your answer with a clear logical flow. Examiners use mark schemes — hit each point.
  • Draw clear, labelled diagrams even when not asked — they often carry marks.
